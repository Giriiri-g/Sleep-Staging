\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $n \geq 0 \vee x \neq 0$
\Ensure $y = x^n$ 
\State $y \Leftarrow 1$
\If{$n < 0$}\label{algln2}
        \State $X \Leftarrow 1 / x$
        \State $N \Leftarrow -n$
\Else
        \State $X \Leftarrow x$
        \State $N \Leftarrow n$
\EndIf

            \State $N \Leftarrow N / 2$
        \Else[$N$ is odd]
            \State $y \Leftarrow y \times X$
            \State $N \Leftarrow N - 1$
        \EndIf
\EndWhile
\end{algorithmic}

\section{Cross referencing}\label{sec8}

Environments such as figure, table, equation and align can have a label
declared via the \verb+\label{#label}+ command. For figures and table
environments use the \verb+\label{}+ command inside or just
below the \verb+\caption{}+ command. You can then use the
\verb+\ref{#label}+ command to cross-reference them. As an example, consider
the label declared for Figure~\ref{fig1} which is
\verb+\label{fig1}+. To cross-reference it, use the command 
\verb+Figure \ref{fig1}+, for which it comes up as
``Figure~\ref{fig1}''. 

To reference line numbers in an algorithm, consider the label declared for the line number 2 of Algorithm~\ref{algo1} is \verb+\label{algln2}+. To cross-reference it, use the command \verb+\ref{algln2}+ for which it comes up as line~\ref{algln2} of Algorithm~\ref{algo1}.

\section{Methods}\label{sec11}

The proposed system for sleep disorder detection processes raw polysomnography recordings through a unified pipeline that produces both sleep stage classifications and disorder predictions with clinical interpretability. Raw EDF files enter through the signal acquisition stage, which extracts EEG, EOG, EMG, ECG, respiratory, and oximetry channels. The preprocessing stage applies anti-alias filtering, downsampling, and epoch segmentation to produce standardized 30-second windows. Feature extraction then operates through two parallel branches. The first branch prepares inputs for automatic sleep staging by computing raw signal tensors and spectral features from EEG channels. The second branch derives clinical features across seven domains including sleep architecture, respiratory events, oxygenation, cardiac activity, and limb movements. The sleep staging module employs a temporal-spectral fusion network that classifies each epoch into five stages: Wake, N1, N2, N3, and REM. The disorder detection module combines clinical features with sleep stage information to predict sleep apnea, insomnia, and restless legs syndrome. An explainability component provides per-prediction interpretability through attention visualization and signal-level event localization. The complete pipeline is illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:methodology}.

\subsection{Datasets}\label{subsec:data}

This study utilizes two complementary PSG datasets.

\subsubsection{Sleep-EDF Expanded v1}

The Sleep-EDF Expanded dataset version 1.0.0 provides annotated PSG recordings for sleep stage classification research. Recordings include EEG channels Fpz-Cz and Pz-Oz sampled at 100 Hz, horizontal EOG, and submental chin EMG. Expert technologists scored sleep stages in 30-second epochs following Rechtschaffen and Kales criteria, later mapped to AASM standard. This dataset serves as the primary source for developing the temporal-spectral fusion sleep staging model.

\subsubsection{MESA Sleep Study}

The Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis Sleep cohort provides comprehensive PSG data linked to clinical assessments for sleep disorder diagnoses. Participants were adults aged 45 to 84 years from six US communities with African American, Hispanic, Asian, and White representation. Full-night attended PSG recordings were acquired between 2010 and 2013 using standardized equipment across all sites.

Recorded signals include three EEG channels F4-M1, C4-M1, O2-M1 at 256 Hz, left and right EOG, submental chin EMG, bilateral leg EMG, single-lead ECG, nasal pressure airflow, oronasal thermistor, thoracic and abdominal effort belts, finger pulse oximetry at 1 Hz, and body position sensors. Certified sleep technologists scored all recordings in 30-second epochs according to AASM criteria.

Clinical diagnoses for three sleep disorders were derived from standardized questionnaire assessments. Sleep apnea status comes from self-reported diagnosis indicating whether participants were told they have sleep apnea. Insomnia status reflects questionnaire responses about chronic sleep initiation and maintenance difficulties. Restless legs syndrome status uses standardized RLS criteria responses.

\subsection{Data Preprocessing}\label{subsec:preprocessing}

The preprocessing pipeline transforms raw EDF files into structured tensors and feature vectors suitable for deep learning and machine learning models.

\subsubsection{Raw Signal Processing}

PSG signals from EDF files undergo channel selection, anti-alias filtering, downsampling, epoch segmentation, and normalization. High-frequency channels including EEG, EOG, EMG, and ECG are prioritized for sleep staging. Respiratory and oximetry channels are reserved for clinical feature computation. High-frequency channels are low-pass filtered at 45 Hz using a zero-phase Butterworth filter to prevent aliasing artifacts. EEG, EOG, and EMG channels are downsampled from 256 Hz to 100 Hz to reduce computational load while preserving clinically relevant frequency content from delta through beta bands. Continuous signals are segmented into non-overlapping 30-second epochs aligned with expert annotations, yielding 3,000 samples per channel at 100 Hz. Per-channel z-score normalization is applied using dataset-wide mean and standard deviation.

\subsubsection{Spectral Feature Extraction}

For each 30-second epoch, spectral features are computed from the EEG channels to complement the raw signal input. Power spectral density is estimated using Welch's method with 4-second Hann windows and 50 percent overlap. Absolute power is integrated across canonical frequency bands using the formula $P_{\text{band}} = \int_{f_{\text{low}}}^{f_{\text{high}}} S(f) \, df$ where $S(f)$ is the PSD estimate. Frequency bands are Delta 0.5 to 4 Hz, Theta 4 to 8 Hz, Alpha 8 to 12 Hz, Sigma 12 to 16 Hz, and Beta 16 to 30 Hz.

Per-stage aggregation computes mean and standard deviation of band powers for each sleep stage, yielding stage-specific spectral signatures. Derived spectral metrics include alpha intrusion ratio measuring alpha divided by delta power in N2 and N3 stages, beta hyperarousal index as mean beta power across NREM sleep, sigma power during N2 as a spindle activity proxy, and spectral entropy as Shannon entropy of the normalized PSD. The spectral feature vector per epoch contains 34 features.

\subsubsection{Clinical Feature Extraction}

From the preprocessed PSG signals and hypnogram, over 130 clinical features are computed across seven domains.

Sleep architecture features include total sleep time, time in bed, sleep onset latency, wake after sleep onset, sleep efficiency computed as total sleep time divided by time in bed expressed as percentage, absolute duration and percentage for each stage, N3 to N2 ratio, combined N1 and N2 divided by N3 ratio, REM latency as time from sleep onset to first REM epoch, total stage transitions, transition rate per hour, transition entropy, mean segment duration, segment variance, and wake interruptions per hour.

Respiratory features are computed following AASM criteria. The apnea-hypopnea index equals the sum of apneas and hypopneas divided by total sleep time in hours. Airflow signals are bandpass filtered from 0.05 to 4 Hz with envelope computed using 3-second RMS windows. Apneas are detected as 90 percent or greater amplitude reduction for 10 seconds or longer. Hypopneas are detected as 30 percent or greater reduction with associated desaturation. Events are classified as obstructive or central based on respiratory effort presence. Features include AHI, AI, HI, OAI, CAI, REM-specific AHI, and mean and maximum apnea duration.

Oxygenation features from SpO2 analysis include mean, minimum, and standard deviation of oxygen saturation, T90 as time with SpO2 below 90 percent, T88 as time with SpO2 below 88 percent, ODI3 and ODI4 as oxygen desaturation indices at 3 percent and 4 percent thresholds, and desaturation AUC as the integral of deficit below 90 percent.

Cardiac and HRV features are computed from ECG using R-peak detection via a Pan-Tompkins-inspired algorithm. Time-domain metrics include SDNN as standard deviation of NN intervals, RMSSD as root mean square of successive differences, and mean RR interval. Frequency-domain metrics include LF power from 0.04 to 0.15 Hz, HF power from 0.15 to 0.40 Hz, and LF divided by HF ratio via interpolated RR series. Stage-specific mean heart rate is computed for each sleep stage.

Periodic limb movement features are derived from leg EMG that is rectified and smoothed with 100 ms windows. Bursts are detected using AASM criteria with amplitude threshold of 8 times baseline, duration from 0.5 to 10 seconds, inter-movement interval from 5 to 90 seconds, and PLM series of 4 or more consecutive movements. Features include PLM count, PLMI as PLM index per hour, burst rate, and IMI statistics.

REM behavior disorder features analyze chin EMG during REM sleep. Tonic activity measures percentage of REM epochs with greater than 30 percent duration above 2 times NREM baseline. Phasic activity counts 0.1 to 5 second bursts above 4 times NREM baseline. Bruxism detects 0.25 to 2 second bursts above 4 times background with Bruxism Episode Index.

\subsubsection{Sleep Stage Sequence Extraction}

The hypnogram from expert annotation provides the ground truth sleep stage sequence for model training. Stages are encoded as integers with Wake equal to 0, N1 equal to 1, N2 equal to 2, N3 equal to 3, and REM equal to 4. Recordings with missing or ambiguous epochs are marked with a special ignore label and excluded from loss computation during training.

\subsection{Data Representation}\label{subsec:representation}

The processed data is organized into two distinct input streams that feed separate downstream modules.

\subsubsection{Dual-Channel Input for Sleep Staging}

The temporal-spectral fusion sleep staging model receives two parallel input channels per epoch. The temporal channel contains raw EEG signal represented as a tensor where $T$ is the number of epochs in the sequence, typically 256, and 3000 represents 30 seconds of EEG at 100 Hz. This channel preserves the complete temporal waveform for learning fine-grained signal patterns. The spectral channel contains pre-computed spectral features where each of the 34 features corresponds to band power statistics and derived spectral metrics for that epoch. This channel provides frequency-domain information that complements the raw signal.

For batch processing, sequences are padded to uniform length and packed into tensors where $B$ is the batch size. The corresponding labels are integers from 0 to 4 representing the five sleep stages.

\subsubsection{Clinical Feature Representation for Disorder Detection}

The disorder detection module receives a structured feature vector per subject with approximately 132 features after preprocessing that prunes near-zero variance features and highly correlated duplicates. The feature vector is organized into seven groups. Sleep architecture contributes 24 features including TST, WASO, SE, stage percentages, latencies, and transition metrics. Respiratory contributes 20 features including AHI, AI, HI, OAI, CAI, apnea durations, and REM-specific indices. EEG spectral contributes 60 features including band powers per stage per channel, derived ratios, and spectral entropy. HRV contributes 7 features including SDNN, RMSSD, LF/HF ratio, and mean HR. Leg movement contributes 6 features including PLM count, PLMI, burst rate, and IMI statistics. REM and bruxism contribute 9 features including tonic and phasic EMG activity and BEI. Oximetry contributes 8 features including SpO2 statistics, T90, T88, ODI3, ODI4, and desaturation AUC.

The clinical feature matrix for the dataset has $N$ subjects and $D$ features. Each subject has three binary disorder labels for apnea, insomnia, and RLS.

\subsubsection{Integration with Sleep Stage Outputs}

The sleep stage sequence produced by the staging module serves as an intermediate representation that informs clinical feature computation. Stage-specific features such as REM-AHI, N3 delta power, and stage-specific heart rate require accurate stage assignments. This creates a dependency where disorder detection quality is partially contingent on sleep staging accuracy. The clinical features themselves are computed from raw signals using the expert-annotated hypnogram during training.
